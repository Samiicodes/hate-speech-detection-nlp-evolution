Error analysis · System 3

Who the Model Actually Flags

RoBERTa over-flags Muslim-targeted content at 2.4× its rate elsewhere. The worry was that it silences people describing the abuse rather than people committing it. Reading all 48 posts says otherwise.

HateXplain test split roberta-base, seed 42 48 of 48 coded — full population

The concern does not hold

3 / 48 posts that silence someone describing or experiencing abuse

Of every non-hateful, Islam-targeted post RoBERTa flags, one is counter-speech and two come from in-group or intra-minority speakers. The other 45 are anti-Muslim content that the annotators graded offensive rather than hatespeech.

So the over-flag gap is not the model policing people who talk about Islamophobia. It is the model drawing the hate/offensive line in a different place than three crowdworkers did, on posts that are hostile either way. That is a weaker and more defensible finding than the raw gap implies — and it is the one the evidence supports.

What was read

Every non-hateful, Islam-targeted post in the test split that roberta_base flags as hate: 48 posts out of a pool of 137. Not a sample — the whole population, which is small enough to read end to end.

Only 4 of the 48 carry the majority label normal; the other 44 are offensive. The original plan was to code ~50 normal false positives, but only four exist, so the population was widened to all non-hateful flagged posts and the original label is reported alongside each one.

Slurs are masked. Posts are otherwise verbatim, including original spelling and missing punctuation. <user> and <number> are HateXplain's own anonymisation tokens.

Coding scheme

Six mutually exclusive categories. Where a post fits two, it is coded by its primary target and function. The last two are the ones the research question is actually worried about.

slur_dehumanisation
Anti-Muslim slur, or a dehumanising generalisation about Muslims as a group — vermin/savages/animal framing, collective accusations of rape or terror.
nativist_political
Hostile political argument about Muslims — deportation, invasion, demographic replacement, banning Islam — carrying no slur and no explicit dehumanisation.
doctrinal_attack
Attack framed on Islam as doctrine or on Muhammad, rather than directly on Muslims as people.
other_group_primary
Hate whose primary target is another group, most often antisemitic, with Muslims mentioned in passing.
ingroup_intraminority
Speaker identifies as Muslim, or as a member of another minority speaking about Muslims.
counter_speech
Naming, criticising or reporting anti-Muslim treatment — the failure mode where a model silences the person describing abuse rather than the person committing it.

Two things separate the paradigms. RoBERTa's 18 extra flags skew toward nativist_political — 6 of 18, a third — against a quarter overall. Those are the slur-free posts arguing for deportation and demographic replacement, exactly the implicit, coded hostility a keyword system cannot see. RoBERTa extends flagging into slur-free hostile speech.

In the other direction, 2 of the 8 posts only the SVM flags (25%) fall in the concerning categories, against 3 of 48 (6%) across all of RoBERTa's. On this small sample the SVM is proportionally worse at the failure that matters: one of its two is a British Muslim criticising other Muslims, the other is someone correcting a conflation of "Arab" and "Muslim".

What this does not establish

  • The sample is small and from one seed. 48 posts, from roberta_base at seed 42. Three posts in the concerning categories is three posts; a different seed could plausibly give one or six.
  • Only 4 flagged posts carry the normal label. The claim that RoBERTa does not disproportionately flag genuinely innocuous Muslim-targeted content rests on 45 such posts in the whole test split. That comparison is underpowered by construction.
  • Coding is single-rater. Categories were assigned by one reader with no second coder and no inter-annotator agreement statistic. The boundary between nativist_political and slur_dehumanisation in particular involves judgement.
  • Nothing here is about under-detection. This reads only what the model flags, not the Muslim-targeted hate it misses — which is the other half of the research question and needs its own pass.
